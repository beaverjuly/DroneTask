Mouse trajectory quality check

An optional end of task measure designed to test whether brief pointer trajectories provide usable data quality information.

Eight target movements After the final memory response No payment or exclusion decisions

Participant experience

What the participant does

  1. Use your mouse or trackpad to click each circle as it appears.
  2. Move at a natural pace. There is no need to rush.
  3. After eight circles, report Mouse, Trackpad, or Other.

Observed in two developer runs
About 10 seconds of clicking and 13–23 seconds from instructions through the device report.

Only one target is visible at a time. Try the deck demonstration to the right.

Acquisition design

Why the sequence is fixed

  • Every participant completes the same eight movements.
  • The route includes short, medium, and long reaches in horizontal, vertical, and oblique directions.
  • Each trajectory begins at one successful click and ends at the next.
  • The geometry is normalized and versioned so results remain interpretable across screens and task releases.

Research inspiration, not replication. We borrow the fixed sequential target structure, use our own geometry, and do not implement the published classifiers.

Shen et al. 2014 and Acien et al. 2022 use fixed sequential targets. Acien reuses Shen's human data. Fitts 1954 motivates varying movement amplitude while all eight targets remain the same size.

Proposed pilot decision

Proposed floor checks

These lab tolerances target distinct failure modes. The cited papers do not validate these cutoffs.

CheckRuleWhy
C1 Completion
  • At least 90% of expected sessions
  • Summary status complete
  • Tests operational feasibility
  • Keeps failures in the denominator
C2 Sampling
  • At least 80% of valid sessions
  • Median across eight paths is at least 20 points
  • Median Δt is 20 ms or less
  • Twenty points support up to 18 heading changes
  • 20 ms equals a median cadence of at least 50 Hz
  • Allows some browser or device loss
C3 Geometry
  • Spearman ρ(distance, path length) > .50
  • Pooled median efficiency < .99
  • Movement amplitude remains visible
  • Excludes nearly perfect straight lines
C4 Synthetic
  • Separation from Type A straight constant speed paths is at least .80 for efficiency or turning
  • Easy positive control for path shape
  • Separation .80 equals AUC .90 in either direction
  • Does not test speed profiles or realistic bots
C5 Sensitivity
  • Absolute Spearman correlation between turning and median sample interval is below .50
  • Correlation must be estimable
  • Checks whether turning follows sampling cadence
  • Missing result is not evaluable
Developer QA
  • Combined data pass all computed checks
  • 16 of 16 paths, no misclicks, and exact duration matching
  • About 62 Hz on Mac Chrome and no added coalesced points
  • Type A separation .99, curved efficiency .13, curved turning .83
Interpretation
  • Floor checks show a real and usable recorded signal
  • They do not show detection of low quality participants
  • C5 needs sample interval variation across browsers and devices

Recommendation

Pilot the measure with clear limits

Use the module as an optional data quality measure. Do not describe or use it as a validated bot detector.

  • Confirm coverageReview consent and IRB language for pointer trajectory recording.
  • Agree on criteriaApprove the proposed floor checks before reviewing group results.
  • Complete QATest mouse, trackpad, and major browsers before the pilot.
  • Reassess after the pilotTest whether trajectory outliers coincide with independent signals such as comprehension-check failures, then weigh any added information against the 13–23 second burden.
